### **Personal Goal 1: BODY: Move first, move after meals, track everything**

Build a body that runs on daily movement, not willpower: out of bed and moving immediately, moving right after every meal, lifting three times a week, fasting once a week, and tracking all of it. By day 100, bring my biomarkers into the healthy/fit range for a 46-year-old man, including blood sugar and belly fat.

**What counts:** Three movement types every day, walk/run, calisthenics, and yoga/stretching, in three chunks:

- **Morning move (the anchor):** out of bed and moving immediately, for 10+ minutes of walk/run, calisthenics, or yoga. Whichever I do is checked off for the day.
- **Post-meal moves:** within 15 minutes of finishing lunch and dinner, 10+ minutes each of the two types I haven't done yet. Walking is the strongest blood-sugar tool, and yoga after a big meal stays gentle. On fast days these happen at midday and evening anyway.
- **Resistance:** weights, machines, or a serious calisthenics session (standard below), at least 3 times a week. It can double as one of the day's chunks.
- **Gym walk-in:** every day I have convenient access to a gym (hotel or home), I walk in. Five minutes counts. Showing up is the habit.
- **Cardio intervals:** Norwegian 4x4s and sprint sessions (protocols below), ramping to 3 a week. A hard session counts as the day's walk/run chunk.
- **Hydration:** a Hydration Day means hitting all four checkpoints (protocol below): two large glasses of ice water on waking before coffee, my daily total sipped through the day, two full glasses before dinner, and rehydration after cardio.
- **Food:** ketosis until dinner every day, and a hard ceiling of 4 carb meals per week (protocol below).
- **Weekly fast:** one 36-hour fast per week (for example, dinner one night to breakfast two mornings later), with plenty of water and electrolytes.
- **Chosen discomfort:** at least 4 times a week, I choose something uncomfortable and stay with it on purpose (protocol below).
- **Supports:** my phone charges outside the bedroom, clothes are laid out the night before, sleep and wake times stay consistent, and meals are mostly real food.

**How I score it:** Daily: morning move (yes/no) and chunks done (0–3). #### **Hydration protocol**

Four checkpoints make a Hydration Day. Floor: 5 of 7 days. Target: every day.

- **On waking:** two large glasses of ice water before coffee.
- **Through the day:** sip to my daily total, tracked in ounces, with pale-yellow urine as the check. I carry a marked 1-liter bottle and count bottles, which works in any city.
- **Before dinner:** two full glasses of water.
- **After cardio:** a large glass right after the session and another within the hour, more on hot days or after sprints.
- **Fast days:** water keeps flowing all day. The fast is from food, not fluids.

#### **Food protocol**

Two rules: ketosis until dinner, and no more than 4 carb meals a week. I don't always control my meals when I'm away from home, so the rules are written for restaurants and other people's kitchens.

- **What makes a carb meal:** a real serving of starch or sugar, such as bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, tortillas, dessert, juice, soda, or beer. Vegetables, nuts, and dairy don't make a meal a carb meal. Judged by eye, not by grams, so it works anywhere.
- **Breakfast and lunch:** protein, fat, and vegetables only. When I'm eating out: order the protein, swap the starch for extra vegetables or salad, go bunless, and skip the bread basket.
- **When a carb meal happens:** it goes at dinner when I have the choice. I eat the protein and vegetables first and the carbs last, then do the post-dinner move with no exceptions.
- **When a meal is out of my control:** I eat around the starch when I can. When I can't, it counts as one of my 4, and I make that post-meal walk a longer one.
- **Ketone spot-checks:** a blood ketone meter a couple of mornings a week, logged, to confirm I'm actually in ketosis. The kit goes in the Week 1 gear list.

#### **Chosen discomfort**

Appreciate discomfort. It counts when I choose it and stay with it on purpose: I notice the urge to quit or escape, breathe slowly, and keep going. That's the same muscle as eating a frog. Floor: 4 a week. Target: every day. It can double-count with a movement chunk, a resistance session, or an interval session.

- **Effort:** calisthenics sets taken to true failure, the last round of a 4x4, or a sprint session.
- **Heat:** walking outside in the heat, or 20+ minutes in the sauna.
- **Cold:** a cold shower (fully cold for the last 2+ minutes) or a cold plunge.
- **Holds:** staying in a stretch or yoga pose that bites for 2+ minutes.
- **Ground:** walking barefoot outside.
- **Hunger:** sitting with hunger during the weekly fast instead of scrolling past it.

#### **Serious calisthenics standard**

A session replaces weights when it's full-body, about 30–45 minutes, with 3 working sets per movement pattern, and every working set ends within 1–2 reps of failure.

- **Push:** push-ups, then decline and archer push-ups. Dips on chairs or bars. Pike push-ups for shoulders.
- **Pull:** rows on a suspension trainer or table edge, then pull-ups and chin-ups, then slow negatives.
- **Legs:** squats, then Bulgarian split squats, then pistol-squat progressions. Single-leg hip thrusts or single-leg deadlifts.
- **Core:** hollow holds, leg raises, side planks.
- **Portable kit:** a suspension trainer and loop bands in the bag; a doorway pull-up bar at home.

#### **Yoga and stretching standard**

A yoga chunk counts when it's 10+ minutes of continuous, breath-led movement or holds. This is the recovery system for everything else, so it targets what travel, laptops, sprints, and calisthenics tighten up: hips, hamstrings, upper back, and shoulders.

- **Morning (energizing):** 3–5 rounds of sun salutations, then a deep squat hold and a few low lunges each side. Good for the anchor move on stiff mornings.
- **After meals (gentle):** cat-cow, low lunges, standing side bends, gentle twists, and slow standing flows. No inversions, deep forward folds, or hard core work on a full stomach.
- **Bedtime wind-down:** longer static holds of 60–90 seconds (pigeon, supported child's pose, reclined twist, legs up the wall). This doubles as the phone-free wind-down that makes the next morning's move easier.
- **Around hard sessions:** dynamic mobility (leg swings, hip circles, arm circles) before intervals and lifting; static holds after, not before.
- **Once a week (target, not floor):** one longer 30–45 minute session, either a class or a follow-along video.
- **Mobility checks at Day 0, midpoint, and end:** the sit-to-rise test, a deep squat hold, and a toe touch (in the readings table). For sit-to-rise: sit down cross-legged on the floor and stand back up without using my hands. I start from 10 points and lose 1 for each hand, knee, or forearm I use for support, and half a point for any wobble.

#### **Cardio intervals**

Hard sessions happen on an incline treadmill, bike, rower, stair climber, pool, or hill rather than flat-ground sprinting. Heart-rate zones start from an estimated max of about 176 bpm (208 − 0.7 × age) and get recalibrated to the highest number I actually see.

- **Norwegian 4x4:** a 10-minute warm-up, then four rounds of 4 minutes at 85–95% of max heart rate (about 150–167 bpm), each followed by 3 minutes of active recovery at about 70% (about 125 bpm), then a 5-minute cool-down. About 40 minutes total. The hard rounds are hard but sustainable, not all-out: if I can't finish round four, round one was too fast.
- **Sprint session:** a 5-minute warm-up, then 20–30 second sprints at max effort, walking until my breathing recovers (usually 2–4 minutes), repeated for about 20 minutes. I stop when sprint speed clearly drops, not when I'm wrecked. Start at 4 sprints and add one a week.
- **Swimming:** counts for either protocol. In the water my max heart rate runs about 10–13 bpm lower than on land, so the 4x4 zones become roughly 140–155 bpm hard and about 115 bpm recovery, and I judge the effort by breathing and stroke rate more than the watch. For the 4x4 the hard rounds are 4 minutes of continuous swimming; for sprints they are 25s and 50s repeats with rest on the wall until my breathing recovers. Zero impact, so it is the option that works when my legs are wrecked or my joints are complaining.
- **Spacing:** no hard sessions on back-to-back days when I can avoid it, and not the day after a heavy leg session.

#### **Tracking setup (Week 1, one evening)**

Set it up once, then leave it alone.

- **Gear:** a blood ketone meter, a smart scale with body composition, a body tape measure, a home blood pressure cuff, and a wearable that estimates VO2 max and resting heart rate. Optional: an over-the-counter continuous glucose monitor for the first two weeks, to see which meals spike me.
- **Labs at Day 0 and at the end:** HbA1c, fasting glucose, a lipid panel with ApoB, and a kidney panel (eGFR and a urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio). HbA1c reflects roughly three months of blood sugar, so a 100-day program times it well. Optional: a DEXA scan at both ends for a direct visceral-fat number.
- **Files:** health_log.csv (one row per day) and readings.csv (one row per measurement).
- **Sunday review:** paste both files into Claude and ask for a summary against my floors and targets, plus the one thing to fix next week.

- **Visual notes:** a page of graphics and diagrams, sparse on text, that distills a stack of cards. Done on evenings I'm not making music, in place of YouTube.

#### **Notecard system**

Cards during the day, not notebooks or devices. Every card gets its loop closed.

- **Capture:** one idea, task, or note per card, carried in a pocket or small case. Cards are disposable by design.
- **Reflect:** lay the cards out and sort them spatially, grouping, sequencing, and tossing.
- **Close the loop, step 1 (scan):** photo-scan every card into one searchable archive.
- **Step 2 (enter):** anything that belongs in a system goes into it: contacts and deal notes into the CRM, ideas and knowledge into my digital corpus.
- **Step 3 (distill):** a page of visual notes, meaning graphics and diagrams with sparse text, that summarizes a stack of cards. 3+ pages a week, on evenings I'm not making music.
- **Weekly Review check:** the card stack is clear, meaning every card is scanned, entered, or tossed.

#### **Frog Morning**

**The vocabulary:**

- **Frog:** a task that should or needs to get done, that I would previously have avoided, and whose delay causes increasing losses. The loss can be money or penalties, and just as often it is trust or network value.
- **Bullfrog:** a frog with real complexity, often vague and ill-defined, that will take a substantial amount of time. Tax filing corrections. Angel and fund portfolio data gathering. Finishing an app so other people can use it. Pooling every LP connection I have ever made across my email into a single CRM.
- **Loose thread:** an obligation to reply or communicate, with no concrete value for me.
- **Loose end:** an obligation that includes an unfinished deliverable.

The test for what counts: I'd rather do almost anything else, there's no payoff or pleasure in it, and it gets worse the longer it sits.

**How each is eaten:** a frog is eaten by doing it, killing it, delegating it, or putting a real date on it. A bullfrog is eaten first by scoping it, meaning broken into a dated sequence of ordinary frogs, and each of those then counts on its own. A loose thread is eaten by replying or declining. A loose end is eaten by delivering it, renegotiating it, or withdrawing it.

**What lands on the list:**

- **Money admin:** accounting, expenses, tax readiness, paying bills and fees.
- **Legal and regulatory:** business registrations, filings, renewals, and other compliance nuts and bolts.
- **Life admin:** making doctor's appointments and their equivalents.
- **Unfinished enthusiasm:** finishing something I started excited about and dropped once the excitement faded. The filmed-and-never-edited screencasts live here, and they get drained on Frog Mornings rather than in new recording sessions.
- **Loose threads and loose ends:** introductions I promised, and feedback on companies, strategies, and investments.
- **Logistics:** planning travel and calendaring.
- **Planning and triage:** triaging my own tasks, task manager, and planning system until every item is done, dated, delegated, or killed. My brain doesn't like planning, logistics, or details, which is exactly what makes this a frog.
- **Doesn't count:** inbox triage with nothing closed, redesigning or reconfiguring my planning system (that's tool-building), new projects, capital conversations, or anything I'd happily do anyway.

#### **Push system**

One artifact out the door each week, on work I have already done. The work is the work; the push finishes it. Everything here is the last mile of something that already exists.

- **What a push is:** something published where my network can see it, with my name on it and no gate in front of it.
- **Many ways count.** A screencast cut from the backlog. A changelog entry promoted to the public surface. A deck timed to my own audio. A visual note page. A written note on something I am ahead of the curve on. A push on a travel day can be the shortest of these.
- **The standard is good enough.** I am confident in the ideas. What I am building is the confidence that a rough cut published beats a polished one that sits. Filler words, false starts, and a plain background are all acceptable.
- **The ceiling is 45 minutes of editing per piece.** Past that it ships as it is or gets cut shorter. The cut list matters more than the polish, and the mechanical work (filler words, captions, background, audio) is automated, so the 45 minutes is spent deciding what to remove.
- **One action, two goals.** A visual note page that gets published counts for the page and the push. A screencast drained on a Frog Morning counts as a frog and a push.
- **Doesn't count:** building the publishing surface, the deck engine, or shopping for editors, which is tool-building under this goal's rules. Drafts that stay private. Reposting someone else's work without adding my own read on it.
- **Not a creator.** No schedule to serve, no format to defend, no audience to grow on purpose. I publish what I was already working on, and I stop when the 12 weeks tell me which format I actually keep doing.
