### One agreement, three motions, and a sequence

The commercial paper does not change across these fourteen categories. A single
VAR agreement — wholesale floor, margin split, support boundary, registrar of
record — covers a documentation engine, a GPU host and an open-source project
alike. **That is an asset, not a simplification:** the reference deal gets
written once and then every conversation starts from a document instead of a
discovery call.

What changes is the motion, and the motion determines the order.

**Community and reference goes first, because it is the precondition.** No
platform will merchandise a namespace that nobody credible uses. A sponsorship
costs a rounding error against a partnership cycle and produces the one thing
the partnership conversation needs: a real, visible, respected project living on
the TLD. `ffmpeg` on an LFG extension is worth more in a Vercel meeting than
any deck. It is also the fastest motion — weeks, not quarters, because a
maintainer decides alone.

**Product-embedded is the revenue.** The first seven rows sell registrations
inside a flow that already exists, which is where the volume argument from the
following sections lives.

**Infrastructure attach is the endgame.** The largest accounts, the most
procurement, and the partners most likely to want proof before they touch it.
Worth opening late and deliberately.

### Two categories that deserve their own note

**Documentation engines are the GEO play in disguise.** Docs and READMEs are
among the most-cited third-party sources in AI answers about technical subjects.
A TLD that appears in the canonical documentation of widely-used tools enters
training corpora and retrieval indexes as a matter of course. That is the
cross-referencing argument from the portfolio section — except run for LFG's own
namespace rather than a customer's brand. Starlight, Mintlify and ReadMe each
publish thousands of docs sites, every one of which resolves at a domain.

**One-click container deploys are the closest thing to a free win.** A
container platform assigns every service a throwaway subdomain at first deploy —
`myapp-production-4f2a.up.railway.app` and its equivalents — and the user's
first instinct on anything they intend to keep is to attach a real domain.
The naming moment is already instrumented, the DNS is already controlled by the
platform, and the upgrade from throwaway to owned is a single obvious step.
[Railway](https://railway.com/) is the notable omission from the toolkit's
current One-Click Deployments tag and belongs at the front of this row.

**GTM automation suites carry the highest domains-per-customer number in the
field.** A campaign microsite per test, per segment, per geography is the
portfolio effect operationalised — and unlike a brand's defensive
registrations, these are bought continuously rather than once. It is the one
category where the per-account domain count is measured in dozens a year rather
than units. [Letterhead](https://letterhead.ai/) sells "10× your newsletters",
which is a domains multiplier stated as a product promise, and
[Ghost](https://ghost.org/) attaches a domain to every publication it hosts —
while also being open source, so it can be reached through either motion.

**Publishing platforms are the proof that this model already works.** Substack
and beehiiv both charge for connecting a custom domain, and Ghost has supported
it since the beginning. That is a domain product, sold inside a creation flow,
to a customer who has just made something they want to name — the exact motion
this proposal describes, running profitably today at companies nobody thinks of
as registrars. The difference LFG can make is inventory: every one of those
platforms currently sends the customer to a third party to buy the name, and
every one of them is selling into a `.com` market where the good names are gone.

[Conductor](https://www.conductor.com/) deserves singling out. It sells itself
as the enterprise platform for **answer engine optimisation** — "where
enterprises go to win in AI search." Its entire product is the argument the
portfolio section makes, sold to exactly the buyers who already fund it. A
partner whose customers are paying to be cited more often by generative engines
is the most natural possible distribution for a namespace whose pitch is more
credible owned properties. That is a first conversation, not a late one.

### The open-source axis

"Open-source sponsorship" is not one category sitting beside documentation
engines and GPU hosts — it is a second axis that cuts across every kind of
software there is. It gets its own table because the unit is a **kind of
project**, not a kind of company, and because each kind maps to a different
part of LFG's namespace.

that can be worked in parallel because no two conversations depend on each
other. The next table narrows this to the pairings sharp enough to be worth
leading with.

### TLDs that already have an obvious partner

The community motion gets vague fast — "sponsor some open source" is not a plan.
It stops being vague the moment you pair a specific TLD with the specific
project everyone already has installed. The pairings below came from auditing
the 185 Homebrew formulae on one working developer's machine, then matching each
tool against LFG's published namespace. **Every TLD in this table is one LFG
has.**

| TLD | The obvious partner | Why it fits | LFG's own tagline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.tldr` | [tldr](https://tldr.sh/) | The name is already the TLD | "Shortcuts for busy lives" |
| `.find` | [ripgrep](https://github.com/BurntSushi/ripgrep), [fzf](https://junegunn.github.io/fzf/) | Search is the entire product | "Find what you didn't know you needed" |
| `.scan` | [Tesseract](https://tesseract-ocr.github.io/) | OCR, literally scanning | "Instant look, instant record" |
| `.tag` | [ExifTool](https://exiftool.org/) | Reads and writes metadata tags | "Tag it, track it, know it" |
| `.list` | [eza](https://eza.rocks/) | A modern `ls` | "Where everything comes together" |
| `.text` | [Helix](https://helix-editor.com/), [Neovim](https://neovim.io/) | Terminal text editors | "Words on the wire" |
| `.source` | [Jujutsu](https://jj-vcs.github.io/jj/) | Source control, and small enough to say yes | "The root of it all" |
| `.file` | [Agent Skills](https://agentskills.io/), [libSQL](https://turso.tech/libsql) | A skill *is* one file you drop in; a libSQL database is one file too | "All your info, one place" |
| `.model` | [Prisma](https://www.prisma.io/), [Drizzle](https://orm.drizzle.team/) | An ORM's job is defining the data model — `model User {}` is literally Prisma's syntax | "The reference point" |
| `.raw` | [ffmpeg](https://ffmpeg.org/), [ImageMagick](https://imagemagick.org/) | Media in its unprocessed form | "Not done yet" |
| `.pod` | [Podman](https://podman.io/), [Zellij](https://zellij.dev/) | Containers and multiplexed panes | "Pack it, keep it, carry on" |
| `.llm` | [Ollama](https://ollama.com/) | Local inference | "Words that work for you" |
| `.key` | [GnuPG](https://gnupg.org/), [OpenSSL](https://openssl-library.org/) | Keys and signing | "Open doors, new possibilities" |
| `.index` | [Atuin](https://atuin.sh/) | Searchable shell history | "Everything in its right place" |
| `.print` | [Pandoc](https://pandoc.org/) | Document conversion | "Create, share, repeat" |

These are not hobby projects — several are among the most widely deployed
software on earth, and the [appendix](#glossary) says what each one is and how
widely it is used.

Several of these are close enough to be memorable on sight. `tldr` on `.tldr` is
an exact match, and `.model` is close behind — Prisma's schema files are written
in a syntax whose keyword *is* `model`. `Tesseract` on `.scan`, `ExifTool` on `.tag` and `SQLite` on
`.file` each describe the tool better than the tool's current domain does.

**`.file` and agent skills is the one unclaimed naming space on this list.**
An [Agent Skill](https://agentskills.io/) is, by design, a single markdown file
you drop into a directory — "it's just a file" is the entire pitch of the
format. The ecosystem is roughly a year old, registries are still forming, and
**no naming convention has settled yet.** Every other pairing in this table
means persuading a project to move from a domain it already has. This one means
being there before the convention exists, which is a different and much cheaper
kind of win.

It is also the category where I have standing rather than a cold introduction:
The Lossless Group maintains its own public
[agent-skills repository](https://github.com/lossless-group/lossless-agent-skills),
and the skills in it are used daily across a twenty-seven repository tree. I can
open these conversations as a participant.

Two things this table is actually for. It converts the community motion from a
budget line into **a list of fourteen named conversations**, each with a
maintainer who can say yes alone. And it is the cheapest credibility in the
plan: a developer who types `brew install` all day meeting `.find` at ripgrep
learns what the namespace is for without reading a word of marketing.

### Use-case anchors and preferred partners

**`.link` is already delegated and already administered by LFG**, which makes it
the one TLD where all of this can happen immediately — no waiting on
evaluation, contention or delegation. Starlink on `.link` is an anchor
conversation available today, and the same is true of every link-in-bio and
short-link platform in the market.

Beyond it, some TLDs have an obvious owner. `.form` belongs in front of
[Typeform](https://www.typeform.com/), [Makeform](https://www.makeform.ai/),
[Jotform](https://www.jotform.com/) and [Tally](https://tally.so/). `.bnb`
belongs in front of [Airbnb](https://www.airbnb.com/) — LFG's own tagline for
the TLD, "wake up somewhere new, every time," is already written to that
use. An anchor of that size does something no marketing spend does: it tells
every subsequent buyer what the TLD is for.

| TLD | Category it defines | Candidate anchors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `.link` **(live today)** | Connectivity and networks | [Starlink](https://www.starlink.com/), link-in-bio and short-link platforms |
| `.form` | Forms and surveys | Typeform, Jotform, Tally, Makeform |
| `.bnb` | Short-term rental | Airbnb, Vrbo, Booking |
| `.stay` | Hospitality | Marriott, Hilton, Hyatt |
| `.ride` | Mobility | Uber, Lyft |
| `.cast` | Podcasting | Spotify, Substack, Transistor |
| `.hire` | Recruiting | LinkedIn, Greenhouse, Ashby |
| `.jet` | Private aviation | NetJets, Wheels Up |
| `.mall` `.sell` | Commerce | Shopify, Etsy |

One more reason anchors matter now: the 2026 round closed with **more than
1,600 applications**. [^61d714] Standing out in a field that size is not a
marketing budget problem. It is a "who famously uses it" problem.
