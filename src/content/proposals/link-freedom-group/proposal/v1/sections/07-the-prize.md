The domain market is 401.6 million names, and .com and .net are 176.1 million
of it — under half, and growing at 3.7% a year. New gTLDs are 52.9 million and
growing at 31.3%, roughly eight times faster. [^93fb63] The category is not the
problem. The question is only how fast a *particular* new extension gets from
delegation to volume, and there the record is unambiguous.

| Extension | Opened | Early traction | Where it got to | How it was sold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `.xyz` | Jun 2014 | 30,000 on day one; 750,000 inside ~6 months | 6M+ by mid-2025 | Registrar promotion, heavy discounting, free giveaways [^2696ef] |
| `.app` | May 2018 | "Hundreds of thousands" in the first 12 months | 750,803 by Jul 2024 | Google's brand, through registrars [^19404b] |
| `.dev` | Mar 2019 | — | 600,000+ over five years | Google's brand, through registrars [^e4de21] |
| `.ai` | ccTLD | — | 1M+ by Jan 2026, ~2,008/day | No channel innovation at all [^73b477] |

### The two lessons, and they point in opposite directions

**`.app` and `.dev` are the warning.** Google — the most credible developer brand
that exists, with Chrome, Android, Search and a captive developer audience —
launched two developer-targeted TLDs and sold them the conventional way. Six
years later `.app` is at 750,000 and `.dev` at 600,000. That is roughly the
ceiling of demand-pull distribution for a developer extension, and Google could
not raise it with brand alone. Worth noting: Google then sold its entire
registrar business — about 10 million domains — to **Squarespace, a website
builder**, for $180M. [^d97e5b] The buyer was a place where people *build things*.

**`.ai` is the proof.** It reached a million registrations and $93M of annual
revenue with no channel program, no platform deals, and a government of 15,000
people running it. It won purely because the TLD matched what everyone was
building at the moment they needed a name.

LFG's bet needs both halves. The namespace supplies the relevance that `.ai`
had. The VAR channel supplies the distribution that `.app` never got.

### Why this is necessity, not upside

At roughly **$1M all-in per application across the ~40 TLDs LFG emphasizes as
the near-term focus, about $40M is committed before a single domain sells.** At a $20 average annual registration,
$40M of yearly gross revenue requires on the order of **two million domains
under management**. `.app` took six years to reach three-quarters of one
million, with Google behind it.

The conventional registrar channel, on its own, does not clear that bar on any
timeline that makes the investment work. That is the real argument for this
engagement. The platform channel is not a clever additional idea — it is the
only distribution model on the table with the shape to produce order-of-magnitude
volume in years one and two, which is precisely when it is needed.
