### Month 1 — precursor planning

A discrete, separately priced phase whose entire job is **converting unknowns
into knowns**. It exists so neither of us commits twelve months to a plan built
on assumptions. Deliverables:

- **Feasibility assessment** — what the registry backend can actually support
  today, what `.link` can carry immediately, and what has to wait for
  delegation.
- **Network-to-partner map** — the target list scored by reachability, with my
  actual routes in named, and the gap between "reachable" and "cold" made
  explicit.
- **Draft VAR agreement** — the reference deal from the integration section
  written as terms, ready to put in front of a partner.
- **Financial projections** — the volume model rebuilt on LFG's real pricing,
  renewal assumptions and margin split, replacing the placeholders in this
  document.
- **The answers to every TK in this proposal**, closed out.

At the end of month 1 you have a plan you can execute with me, execute with
someone else, or decline. That is deliberate — a precursor phase that can only
conclude "hire me for a year" is not a real assessment.

### Months 2–13 — the engagement

Twelve months assumed, with three gates:

| Gate | When | The question it answers | If the answer is no |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **First** | Month 2 | Is the thesis holding in real conversations? Have the first partner meetings produced live interest rather than politeness? | Stop cheaply, having spent three months total |
| **Second** | Month 6 | Is the motion repeating? Signed agreements accumulating on one standard paper, integrations shipping in parallel, something live on `.link` | Re-scope the motion or the target tier |
| **Third** | Month 10 | Is coverage on track for a launch where the channel is live across the category, with attach data strong enough to carry the volume tiers? | Decide year two on evidence rather than optimism |

Each gate is a decision point with a defined exit, not a status meeting. The
month-2 gate matters most: it is the cheapest possible place to find out the
channel thesis does not survive contact with real partnerships leads.

### How I am paid

| Component | What it is | What it rewards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Retainer** | Monthly fee across the twelve months | The work that happens whether or not a deal closes — thesis, materials, list, conversations |
| **Closing bonus** | A fee per partnership agreement signed | Signatures, which is the actual deliverable |
| **Channel participation** | A share of registration revenue originated through partnerships I closed, descending **5% in year one, 4%, 3%, 2%, 1% in year five** | Revenue arriving early, and partnerships that produce rather than merely sign |
| **Expenses** | Conferences, travel, entertainment and business-development activity, reimbursed at cost — plus the ability to procure and use my own tools without a procurement cycle | Nothing, deliberately. It is cost, not incentive |

### Expenses, and where the line sits against the opening

The opening section says the cast comes out of my pocket. That is true and it
stays true — **people are mine; getting in front of partners is not.**

This channel closes at conferences, on flights, and over dinners. A partnerships
lead at Replit or Bubble signs after meeting someone, not after receiving a
deck, and the tier-1 cohort in particular is reached at the events they already
attend. Treating that as overhead I absorb would quietly convert an in-person
job into an email job, and the email version of this engagement does not work.
Reimbursed at cost, with whatever approval threshold you want above a sensible
figure.

The tools clause matters more than it sounds. The claim in the API section —
that a production demo is a months project rather than a years one — rests on a
specific way of working with specific tooling, and that stack changes every few
months. I need to be able to buy it and use it in the week I need it, rather
than put it through a procurement cycle that outlasts its usefulness.

### Why the participation descends

A flat percentage pays the same whether a partnership produces in month three
or month thirty. A descending one does not, and that is the point: **every
percentage point I lose to time is a reason to get partners live and selling
sooner.** It aligns me to exactly what you said this channel is for — driving
year one and year two revenue, not building a pipeline that matures after the
TLDs have already launched quietly.

It also means my compensation decays on its own. By year five my participation
is nominal and the channel is yours outright, with no renegotiation and no
argument about a legacy override.
