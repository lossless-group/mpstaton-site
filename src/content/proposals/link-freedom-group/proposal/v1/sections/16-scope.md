### Phase 1 — Positioning and materials

The deliverable is a **channel pitch that survives being forwarded**: the thesis
in "The wedge" and "The prize", rendered as a one-pager per archetype, plus the
reference deal from "What a VAR integration actually is" written as terms
rather than intentions. Also the target
list with named owners and routes in, and an honest read of what each will
object to.

This phase also resolves the TKs in this document. The pitch cannot be finished
while the delegation timeline, the registry backend, and the flagship TLDs are
open questions, because all three are the first things a serious partner asks.

### Phase 2 — Saturate the fast tiers

Work the agentic builders and the no-code long tail **in parallel, not in
sequence**. These are small teams where a partnerships decision is two
conversations deep, and there are dozens of them — which makes this the tier
where coverage is actually achievable inside a year rather than a multi-year
enterprise slog.

The target is the category, not a sample: **every tier-1 name worth having, and
a majority of the no-code and publishing platforms**, on one standard agreement.
Publishing is the softest ground of all, because Substack and beehiiv already
charge for custom domains — those conversations start with a company that has
already decided domains are a product.

What comes out of this phase beyond the signatures: the first real attach data.
How many users, offered a fresh namespace at the naming moment, take it. That
number is the entire argument for the larger platforms, and nobody currently has
it.

### Phase 3 — The volume tiers

Take the attach data into deploy and hosting, design and site builders, the
documentation engines and the GTM suites — the tiers with the largest user bases
and the longest procurement. These are the accounts that turn coverage into
volume, and they are winnable in year one only because phase 2 arrives carrying
evidence rather than a thesis.

Cloudflare goes last, by design. By this phase the pitch is no longer an idea,
which changes both who takes the meeting and what can be asked for in it.


> [!note] What I am not proposing to do
> Run registrar accreditation, negotiate the registry backend contract, handle
> ICANN process, or build the API. That is registry work and it needs registry
> people. This engagement is the channel: thesis, list, terms, and signatures.
