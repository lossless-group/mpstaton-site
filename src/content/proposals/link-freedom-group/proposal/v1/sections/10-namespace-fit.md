### Portfolio additions, defensive squats: replaces subdomains

Every one of these replaces a subdomain a company is already running. The
TLD does not create a new need — it **reverses an existing one**, and reads
better in the reversal.

| TLD | The subdomain it replaces | Becomes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `.form` | `forms.insurance.com` | `insurance.form` |
| `.auth` `.pass` | `auth.company.com` | `company.auth`, `company.pass` |
| `.demo` | `demo.company.com` | `company.demo` |
| `.sign` | `sign.company.com` | `company.sign` |
| `.feed` | `feed.company.com` | `company.feed` |
| `.era` | — | the wordplay buys: `course.era`, `cloud.era` |

This is the strongest demand argument in the namespace, and it is the least
speculative. **A company does not have to be persuaded it needs a login page or
an intake form — it already has one, at a subdomain, today.** The offer is a
cleaner address for a thing that exists, plus the certainty that a competitor
or a squatter cannot take the brand's own name on that TLD.

Two consequences follow. The buyer is the brand and its registrar rather than a
platform, so this revenue arrives through the portfolio motion and not the
channel — **a separate line with a separate sales motion.** And the
domains-per-customer number stops being an estimate: a mid-size company runs
somewhere between five and fifteen subdomains of exactly this kind, so the
multiplier in the portfolio section has a floor that can be counted rather than
modelled.

`.pass` is the exception that earns its place twice. Alongside the auth use it
has a real consumer destination — **memberships and ticket sales** — which makes
it the only TLD in this group a company would buy to put in front of
customers rather than to keep off the market. It should be pitched into both
motions.

It also renews with near-certainty. A defensive registration is never cancelled
while the brand exists, and one that is carrying live traffic at
`insurance.form` is not cancelled at all.
