  - title: "1 · It closes a hole they already have"
    body: "Custom-domain attach is a known conversion and retention lever on every one of these platforms, and today every one of them ends that flow by sending the user to somebody else. They capture none of the revenue — and still absorb the support tickets when the records come back wrong, because the failure surfaces on their site, not the registrar's."
  - title: "2 · It is differentiation they cannot buy elsewhere"
    body: "Any platform can resell .com. A namespace under LFG's control can be positioned, merchandised, and priced as that platform's own — a curated set of extensions that fit its audience, with availability nobody else can offer."
  - title: "3 · The unit economics are clean"
    body: "Registration and renewal are recurring, high-margin, and already inside the platform's billing relationship. No new payment surface, no new support burden if the registry-of-record sits behind an API."
  - title: "4 · Agents make naming a volume problem"
    body: "An AI app builder generates names continuously and needs them to resolve. A namespace with near-total availability is infrastructure for that, not an upsell — and the platform that wires it first sets the default."

The fourth argument is the one I would lead with at Replit, Lovable, Bolt, v0 and
the rest of the agentic-builder cohort, because for them naming is not a
merchandising opportunity, it is **a bottleneck in the core loop**. Everywhere
else, lead with the first.

> [!note] What they will push back on
> Three objections, every time: *who is the registrar of record and who eats
> support*; *what happens if the TLD is contested or never delegates*; and
> *what do we tell a user who wants a `.com`*. The reference deal structure in
> "What a VAR integration actually is" has to answer all three in writing before the first meeting, or the
> first meeting becomes the only meeting.
